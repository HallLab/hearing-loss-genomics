# `ZNF175` — the declared target, reported whatever it says

**Run 2026-10-15.** [`PREMISES_tinnitus.md`](PREMISES_tinnitus.md) P4 committed this
gene's p-value to a named section in all six tinnitus cells, written before the arm ran
so the result could not be chosen after seeing it. This is that section. The hearing-loss
arms are included as comparators, for eighteen cells in all.

## Why this gene

Park et al. 2021 (*Nature Medicine*) reported `ZNF175` associated with **tinnitus** in an
earlier PMBB release, and the mouse ortholog `Zfp719` produces hearing loss when
disrupted. That pairing is why this project exists. It is **not** in Hui et al. 2023.

Until this arm ran, no part of this project had tested tinnitus — the four earlier arms
all test hearing loss, so the hypothesis of origin had never been examined with the
corrected pipeline.

## What this does and does not establish

**The hypothesis does not hold at this cohort size.** Nothing reaches any bar; the best
of the eighteen cells is p = 0.014 against a bar of 5e-8, and it is in the *bilateral*
arm, not the tinnitus one.

**And this is not a coverage failure.** That matters, because every other null in this
project is explained by how little there is to test — the median gene carries 178 rare
alleles in the tinnitus combined arm. `ZNF175` carries **587**, better covered than
about three quarters of the exome:

| cohort | MAC | median gene | smallest OR detectable at 80% power |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| tinnitus combined | 587 | 178 | 2.77 |
| tinnitus EUR | 190 | 100 | 5.95 |
| tinnitus AFR | 256 | 36 | 4.55 |

So for the combined arm the statement is sharper than "underpowered": **if `ZNF175`
carried a rare-variant effect on tinnitus of OR ≥ 2.8, this study had an 80% chance of
seeing it and did not.** Smaller effects remain entirely possible and are not addressed.

**The direction was pre-recorded.** P4 noted in advance that the original run's delivered
tables put `ZNF175` *better* on hearing loss (p = 0.040 combined, 0.022 EUR) than on
tinnitus (0.331 / 0.139 / 0.064) — the wrong way round for the hypothesis. Our corrected
burden run does not reproduce that ordering either way: tinnitus is nominally better than
broad here (0.696 vs 1.000 combined), but both are so far from any bar that the
comparison carries no weight.

**P6's test is not met.** A tinnitus result is interesting only if it beats the
hearing-loss arms, since 66% of the cases are shared. Nothing here beats anything.
